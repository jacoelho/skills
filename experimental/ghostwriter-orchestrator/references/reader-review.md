# Reader-review facet

## Purpose and inputs

Diagnose what an unfamiliar reader can understand from the draft itself.
Receive only the frozen draft, the intended audience, and the requested genre.
Do not receive the outline, original notes, source/disposition record, writer's
explanations, or other reviewers' findings. Do not retrieve them.

With isolated context this is a draft-only review. In the same conversation it
is a self-review: prior knowledge remains available. Use the same checks, but
do not describe it as blind or independent.

## What to inspect

Start by stating the argument the text actually makes, in a few sentences.
For a passage, identify its claim and its relationship to the surrounding text.
For a long article, outline sections; inspect individual paragraphs only where
needed. This is a reader-facing interpretation, not a request to expose private
reasoning.

Report a defect when you can point to its effect on understanding:

- An unstated premise is needed to connect two claims.
- A pronoun or general term has no clear referent.
- A term is used before the intended audience can understand it.
- Too many claims, definitions, or qualifications arrive before the main point.
- A sentence names abstractions but leaves the reader unsure what happens.
- A transition asserts a relationship the text has not established.
- Grammar, word choice, or sentence structure creates a wrong or ambiguous
  reading; identify the actual ambiguity, not just a vague feeling.
- A section repeats a point without advancing the explanation.

Do not silently repair the missing connection in your interpretation. State
what the draft establishes and where it stops. Do not infer what the author
"must have meant" from familiarity with the topic.

Density is contextual. A long sentence can be clear to its audience; a short
one can hide an unexplained mechanism. Do not use sentence length, jargon
counts, readability grades, or a preference for short outputs as verdicts.

## Check what the explanation enables

For a difficult explanatory passage, identify a question raised by the text
or required by the user's explicit reader task. Cite the wording creating that
expectation and supporting the answer. Do not invent an extra teaching objective
or answer from your own subject knowledge. A mechanism, comparison or practical
step can matter more than how many claims are present.

For a whole article, check progression between sections rather than applying
this exercise to every paragraph. Preserve the requested genre, voice and
reader's assumed knowledge: a narrative scene or deliberate ambiguity does not
need an added lesson. Useful signposting is not production residue.

## Findings, not competing prose

Use [the findings template](../assets/review-findings.md). Quote the exact
passage, describe the reader difficulty, and name the repair needed. Do not
rewrite the whole article or prescribe missing facts. Recommend unpacking,
reordering, defining, connecting, or cutting a detour only when the quoted
passage warrants it.

No minimum number of findings applies. Return "No material reader-facing
issues found" when that is your actual assessment. Do not manufacture edits
to prove that the review happened. A short interpretation plus findings is
sufficient; no score out of ten and no generic praise.

You are not the factual verifier or the authority on the author's intent.
Flag a suspected factual issue for the fidelity reviewer without declaring it
proven from this packet alone. Keep all review output outside the article.
