# Drafting facet

## Purpose and input boundary

Write a coherent article from the selected material and editorial directions.
Use the clean packet, relevant supporting excerpts, and supplied voice
reference. Do not consult rejected drafts or the editing conversation to add
substance. A fresh writer should receive only this packet and its task.

The packet controls the intended content; it does not make unsupported claims
true. Do not invent statistics, studies, quotations, causal explanations, or
first-person experiences. Use an obviously hypothetical example only when
permitted, and present it as hypothetical. Ask the controller for a missing
source or premise instead of quietly manufacturing one; in a single-agent
workflow, record and handle that gap outside the prose.

## Explain before compressing

Let the reader understand the main point before adding secondary implications.
Choose a sequence suited to the genre; do not force every article into a
thesis-example-caveat template. For explanatory writing, useful checks are:

- Name the actor and action when an abstraction hides what happens.
- Introduce a term where it becomes useful; preserve necessary technical terms.
- Make a condition visible before, or alongside, the consequence it limits.
- Explain a relationship rather than masking a missing premise with a
  transition such as "therefore".
- Give a paragraph a recognisable purpose without requiring one sentence or
  exactly one idea per paragraph.

A necessary explanation may become longer when unpacked. Remove duplicate
claims and irrelevant detours before trying to reduce its length. Honour a
hard word limit by prioritising the requested substance, not by deleting
conditions or stuffing several claims into noun-heavy sentences.

Prefer a concrete example when it clarifies the existing argument. Do not add
an example merely to follow a formula, and do not let an analogy imply more
than the selected evidence supports.

## Preserve explanatory work

Keep the supporting step that makes a conclusion understandable. Mentioning
both ends of a relationship is not enough when the reader cannot connect them.
Use the brief's progression notes where supplied. Preserve the function of
necessary support, not a compulsory example count or exact sentence shape.
An optional illustration may be cut or, when authorised, replaced by an equally
useful one. Source evidence, measurements, quotations and personal experience
cannot be replaced by invented support. Do not reduce an explanation to a slogan.
Do not print working questions or annotations unless the article needs them.

You may articulate a connection entailed by the supplied premises. Do not add
a new factual premise, invent a cause, or present a plausible explanation as
established. Keep a substantive inference distinguishable from observation.
When a missing fact is needed, request it through the controller or record
the limitation separately; ordinary connective prose needs no provenance form.

## Preserve voice without copying a template

Use the author's supplied samples for cadence, vocabulary, directness, and
level of explanation. Do not borrow their facts, personal anecdotes, or unusual
phrases for another subject. A voice reference does not authorise stronger
claims. Without samples, use natural, precise prose appropriate to the brief;
do not invent a recognisable authorial persona.

Keep useful repetition of technical terms. Do not replace the same concept
with several synonyms to make the prose appear varied. Vary sentence length
when it helps the explanation rather than making every sentence short.

## Prevent discussion leakage

Editorial feedback changes the draft without being narrated. "Make it
practical" is not a reason to write "this is a practical rather than academic
approach". "Leave this topic for later" usually means omit it, not introduce
it and announce that it is out of scope.

Scope statements, caveats, and discussion of writing are allowed when they
serve the reader and belong to the selected argument. Judge their purpose,
not whether they contain a supposedly forbidden phrase.

## Long pieces

Keep the full argument available while writing sections. Maintain continuity
of definitions, examples, stance, and transitions. Do not draft isolated
sections in parallel and assume concatenation will produce one voice. Preserve
approved passages unless a specific defect or changed requirement justifies
editing them.

## Output

Return the draft only in the requested format. Do not append notes about
following the brief or ask the reader what to write next. Supply a material
limitation separately to the controller rather than concealing it in prose.
