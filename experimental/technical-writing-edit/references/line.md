# Line editing guide

Use this reference only for `line` and `full` modes.

## Actors and verbs

- Name the actor when ownership or mechanism matters.
- Prefer direct verbs over abstract-noun chains: use `evaluate` instead of `perform an evaluation of`.
- Use passive voice only when the actor is genuinely irrelevant.
- Replace unsupported adjectives such as `robust`, `scalable`, `simple`, or `seamless` with the supported mechanism or consequence already present.

## Sentences and paragraphs

- Keep one principal claim per sentence.
- Split a sentence when a second claim requires separate evidence.
- Keep qualifications close to the clause they constrain.
- Avoid pronouns with more than one plausible antecedent.
- Preserve a longer sentence when it expresses one relationship more accurately than several fragments.

## Epistemic and causal language

- Use the canonical claim kinds from `style-guide.md` without substituting synonyms.
- Do not write `will` for a forecast, `must` for a preference, or `observation` for a report.
- Check every `because`, `therefore`, `so`, and equivalent transition against the existing evidence. If support is missing, return a finding rather than smoothing the gap.
- Preserve conditions, exceptions, ownership, chronology, risk severity, and evidence attachment.

## Terminology and punctuation

- Introduce an acronym on first use unless the intended audience already treats it as a canonical term.
- Do not invent jargon or acronyms merely to shorten a local phrase.
- Prefer full stops or colons when an em dash only joins independent claims.
- Remove formulaic transitions, repeated sentence openings, rhetorical questions, and decorative emphasis when meaning is unchanged.

Do not claim proof of semantic equivalence. Return semantic cautions for any edit whose exact meaning cannot be established.
