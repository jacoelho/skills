# Voice and Rhythm

Detect unjustified regularity and drift from established author voice. Do not seek randomness. When voice samples exist, use them as the primary reference.

Inspect sentence openings, clause patterns, local sentence/paragraph lengths, punctuation, transition frequency, parentheticals, rhetorical questions, contractions, formality, function words, and repeated rhetorical constructions.

Investigate runs such as “This allows / This enables / This ensures”, repeated “not X, but Y”, or repeated scaffolding such as “The key is”, “At its core”, “In practice”, “That said”, “Ultimately”, “This is where”, “The result is”. No isolated phrase is intrinsically defective.

Flag sentences whose main purpose is telling the reader how to interpret adjacent prose—announcing importance, nuance, complexity, a takeaway, or broader lesson—when the underlying point can simply be stated.

Sentence form should follow sentence function. Similar sentences may intentionally form a sequence. Flag regularity only without semantic reason.

With samples, compare cadence, directness, punctuation, formality, paragraph construction, and characteristic vocabulary. Distinguish topic-driven differences from voice drift. Do not copy errors or manufacture quirks.

Mentally read aloud; flag stretches whose cadence is predictably uniform or where every sentence carries equal rhetorical weight.
