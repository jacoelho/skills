# Argument Shape

Determine whether structure follows the argument or the argument has been poured into a reusable template. Do not line-edit.

Map each paragraph to its dominant function: claim, evidence, mechanism, example, qualification, objection, consequence, judgement, transition, summary.

Test for delayed claims; mechanical claim→explanation→qualification sequences; unjustified rules of three; similarly sized sections; matched benefits/drawbacks; obligatory counterarguments; equal treatment of unequal issues; paragraph endings that restate openings; bodies that paraphrase headings.

Symmetry is not defective when the subject supplies it. Require semantic justification, not deliberate asymmetry.

Find places where available evidence permits useful judgement but prose retreats into “both have advantages”, “it depends”, “several factors”, or “a balance must be struck”. Never invent a judgement; report missing author position instead.

A conclusion should answer, decide, derive a consequence, expose remaining uncertainty, or narrow disagreement. Flag conclusions whose main job is recap.

Report structural causes and reader costs, not lexical preferences.
