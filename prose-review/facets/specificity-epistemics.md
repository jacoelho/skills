# Specificity and Epistemics

Determine whether claims are bounded enough to understand/dispute and whether confidence matches evidence.

Investigate generic evaluative words such as important, significant, valuable, powerful, effective, robust, key, crucial, meaningful, considerable. Do not ban them. Ask: important to whom? significant relative to what? effective at what? robust against which failure? valuable under which constraint?

Mentally substitute an unrelated subject. If the sentence remains plausible with minimal changes, inspect it for generic content.

For causal claims ask “by what mechanism?” Recommendations: “under what conditions?” Comparisons: “on which dimension?” Predictions: “based on what evidence?”

Audit may, might, could, arguably, potentially, generally, often, typically, likely, perhaps, appears, suggests. Identify the uncertainty: measurement, sampling, causality, incomplete evidence, external validity, forecast uncertainty, disagreement, etc. Flag hedges without an identifiable purpose and certainty stronger than evidence permits.

Ask whether the passage contains concrete propositions a knowledgeable reader could disagree with.

Never strengthen claims for liveliness or invent evidence, numbers, mechanisms, examples, or certainty.
