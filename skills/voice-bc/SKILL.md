# BC voice

Use only when explicitly requested. Read [the voice card](references/voice-card.md) first.

Voice is a set of writing mechanics, not a set of beliefs, topics or phrases. It never overrides facts, uncertainty, ownership or the user's constraints. When `technical-writing` also applies, its contract outranks this skill.

## Operations

- **Write:** build the reasoning from the user's facts, then apply the voice to how the claims relate before touching diction.
- **Rewrite:** preserve claims, certainty, required terms and speaker authority. Rebuild the structure when it is weak; do not swap synonyms.
- **Edit:** make the smallest changes that materially improve the fit. Keep compatible sentences.
- **Review:** return findings with concrete corrections. Do not rewrite unless asked. Look for distinctions without consequences, detached tone where direct judgment is warranted, forced devices and over-imitation.

## Procedure

1. Identify the audience, purpose, mode and factual constraints.
2. Establish what the speaker is entitled to know, recommend, decide or announce.
3. Find the distinction that changes the result, and for whom. If the material has none, do not invent one.
4. Draft for meaning first.
5. Apply the voice card at natural frequency. Do not force signature devices.
6. Check that every qualification sits beside its claim and that nothing unsupported was added.
7. Revise once, with restraint.

## Hard boundaries

- Do not invent first-person experience, relationships, beliefs, biography or historical participation.
- Do not infer the user's positions from the voice.
- Do not manufacture an antagonist, motive or outrage.
- Do not copy source wording, anecdotes, metaphors, openings, endings or outlines.
- Do not claim another author wrote or endorsed the output.

Return only the requested prose unless the user asks for review or explanation. When `technical-writing` applies, its Output section governs.
